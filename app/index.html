How we check a deal

  1. Find it at the store. Every price here was read in the store’s own Black Friday announcement or in a deals source that names the store — never guessed, never from a price we invented.
  2. Check the “was” price. A discount only counts if the usual price is real. If a store raised its list price first, the verdict says so.
  3. Give a verdict. TRUE DEAL means an all-time low or a deep cut on a real list price. GOOD PRICE means a solid cut, near the lowest tracked. ROUTINE SALE means a small cut — buy it for the product, not the discount.
  4. Link the live listing. Every “View at” button opens the store’s search for that exact product, so you see the live price before you buy. Prices move hourly on Black Friday.

Not a deal — read this first

Apple’s own Black Friday event (Nov 27–30) is not a price cut. Apple charges full price and gives you a gift card of up to $300 instead. On a MacBook Air M5, Amazon’s $310 off beats Apple’s $200 gift card — buy it at Amazon. And the new Apple TV 4K at $289 is only $10 off $299: a fine product, a routine sale.
